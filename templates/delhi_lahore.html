Executive Summary

This pioneering cross-border study compares urban green spaces and their cooling effects in Delhi, India, and Lahore, Pakistan - two of South Asia's largest and most rapidly urbanizing cities. Despite different geographical contexts and urban planning approaches, both cities face similar challenges of urban heat islands and green space reduction.

Climate & Environmental Analysis

Temperature Patterns

Delhi experiences more extreme temperatures but benefits from larger green spaces in central areas, while Lahore shows more uniform temperature distribution with localized heat islands.

Rainfall Impact

Delhi receives 797mm annual rainfall supporting denser vegetation, compared to Lahore's 629mm, affecting green space maintenance and cooling efficiency.

Air Quality

Both cities face severe air pollution, but Delhi's larger green spaces provide 15% better air filtration capacity despite higher pollution levels.

Key Findings

Similarities

Urban Expansion Patterns

Both cities show similar patterns of green space loss at urban peripheries due to expansion.

Cooling Benefits

Similar magnitude of temperature reduction (3-4°C) in well-maintained green spaces.

Policy Challenges

Common challenges in enforcement and maintenance of green infrastructure.

Differences

Spatial Distribution

Delhi has more centralized large parks, Lahore has more distributed smaller green spaces.

Administrative Approach

Delhi has more integrated planning, Lahore has fragmented departmental responsibilities.

Climate Context

Different rainfall patterns affect vegetation types and maintenance requirements.
