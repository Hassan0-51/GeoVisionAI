Evaluating the Cooling Effects of Urban Green Spaces Using Remote Sensing and Deep Learning: A Case Study of Lahore, Pakistan

Abstract

This research presents a comprehensive framework for evaluating the cooling effects of Urban Green Spaces (UGS) in Lahore, Pakistan, using advanced remote sensing techniques and deep learning models. With rapid urbanization contributing to increased Urban Heat Island (UHI) effects, there is an urgent need for data-driven approaches to urban planning and climate resilience.

We developed and trained multiple deep learning architectures, including U-Net and DeepLabv3+, on Sentinel-2 satellite imagery to accurately segment and classify green spaces within Lahore's metropolitan area. The segmentation results were correlated with Land Surface Temperature (LST) data derived from MODIS and Landsat 8 thermal bands to quantify the cooling impact of vegetated areas.

Our findings reveal a significant inverse correlation between green space density and surface temperature, with well-maintained parks and tree-lined streets showing up to 3.2°C cooling compared to adjacent built-up areas. The study also identifies critical gaps in green space distribution across Lahore and provides evidence-based recommendations for urban planners and policymakers.

1. Introduction

Lahore, Pakistan's second-largest city with a population exceeding 13 million, has experienced rapid urbanization over the past two decades. This growth has come at the cost of green spaces, exacerbating urban heat island effects and reducing the city's climate resilience. The average temperature in Lahore has increased by 1.5°C over the last 30 years, with heatwaves becoming more frequent and intense.

Urban Green Spaces (UGS) play a crucial role in mitigating UHI effects through various mechanisms including evapotranspiration, shading, and albedo modification. However, traditional methods of UGS assessment are labor-intensive, subjective, and lack integration with temperature data. This research addresses these limitations by developing an automated, AI-driven framework for UGS evaluation.

1.1 Research Objectives

  1. Develop a deep learning pipeline for automatic segmentation of urban green spaces from satellite imagery
  2. Quantify the correlation between green space density and land surface temperature in Lahore
  3. Create a web-based platform for stakeholders to visualize and analyze UGS cooling effects
  4. Provide evidence-based recommendations for urban planning and climate resilience

2. Methodology

1

Data Collection

Sentinel-2 imagery (10m resolution) for Lahore metropolitan area (2020-2023). MODIS and Landsat 8 thermal data for LST calculation.

2

Data Preprocessing

Cloud masking, atmospheric correction, and radiometric calibration using Google Earth Engine API.

3

Model Training

U-Net and DeepLabv3+ architectures trained on 5,000 annotated images. 80-10-10 train-validation-test split.

4

Analysis

Spatial correlation between green space density and LST. Statistical analysis using Python (scikit-learn, pandas).

3.2 Temperature Analysis

Our analysis revealed a strong negative correlation (r = -0.82) between green space density and land surface temperature. Areas with dense vegetation showed consistently lower temperatures, with the cooling effect most pronounced during summer months (May-August).

4. Discussion

4.1 Implications for Urban Planning

The findings have significant implications for urban planning in Lahore and similar rapidly urbanizing cities:

  • Strategic Green Space Placement: Our heat maps identify priority areas for new green infrastructure
  • Tree Species Selection: Analysis suggests certain native tree species provide greater cooling benefits
  • Maintenance Prioritization: Well-maintained parks showed 40% greater cooling effect than neglected ones

4.2 Limitations

Cloud Cover: Sentinel-2 images were occasionally affected by cloud cover, requiring temporal compositing.

Seasonal Variation: Vegetation indices show significant seasonal variation affecting year-round analysis.

Ground Truth Data: Limited availability of high-resolution ground truth data for model validation.

5. Conclusion

This research successfully developed an AI-powered framework for evaluating urban green spaces and their cooling effects in Lahore. The developed platform provides urban planners, policymakers, and researchers with actionable insights for evidence-based decision making.

Key contributions include:

  1. A high-accuracy deep learning model for green space segmentation (94.2% IoU)
  2. Quantification of cooling effects across different green space types
  3. An accessible web platform for interactive analysis
  4. Policy recommendations for Lahore's urban development
