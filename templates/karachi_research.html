Project Overview

This doctoral research project investigates the complex relationship between urban green spaces and heat island effects in Karachi, Pakistan's largest coastal metropolis. The study focuses on how coastal influences moderate temperature patterns and how green infrastructure can be optimized for maximum cooling benefits in a marine-urban environment.

Coastal Context

Karachi's unique coastal location creates distinct microclimates that interact with urban heat island effects. The research examines how sea breezes, humidity, and coastal vegetation influence temperature patterns across different parts of the city.

Research Objectives

1

Quantify Green Coverage

Measure green space distribution across Karachi's 18 towns using satellite imagery analysis.

2

Analyze Temperature Patterns

Map urban heat islands and correlate with green space density, considering coastal influences.

3

Assess Coastal Effects

Evaluate how proximity to the Arabian Sea affects cooling benefits of green spaces.

4

Optimize Green Infrastructure

Develop recommendations for climate-resilient urban planning in coastal cities.

Analysis Approach

The research employs a mixed-methods approach combining quantitative spatial analysis with qualitative field observations. Deep learning models (U-Net architecture) were trained on Karachi-specific imagery to account for local vegetation types and urban patterns.

Spatial Patterns

The analysis revealed significant spatial disparities in green space distribution and cooling effects:

  • Coastal Areas: Higher green coverage (18.2%) with enhanced cooling from sea breezes
  • Central Business District: Lowest green coverage (6.8%) with highest UHI intensity
  • Eastern Industrial Zones: Limited vegetation and severe heat stress
  • Northern Suburbs: Moderate green coverage but limited cooling due to inland location

Coastal-Specific Insights

Unique Coastal Dynamics

Sea Breeze Penetration

Green spaces within 5km of coast show 40% greater cooling than inland equivalents.

Humidity Interaction

High humidity reduces evapotranspiration cooling but enhances shade benefits.

Salt Tolerance

Coastal vegetation requires salt-tolerant species for sustainable greening.

Policy Recommendations

Coastal-Centric Urban Planning

1. Coastal Green Corridors

Develop continuous green spaces along the coastline to maximize sea breeze penetration.

2. Salt-Tolerant Species

Prioritize mangroves and other salt-resistant vegetation for sustainable greening.

3. Wind Channel Planning

Design urban layouts that facilitate sea breeze flow into the city interior.
